How it works

Set up in seconds.

Three small steps, and your accounts stay apart for good.

  1. 1Make a box

    Create a box for each account you use: give it a name, a colour and an icon. “Work”, “Personal”, “Client A”.

  2. 2Pick a box for a tab

    Choose a box in the popup, or let BoxBox ask you before the page loads. The tab opens in its own isolated session.

  3. 3Stay logged in, separately

    Every box keeps its own logins. Come back tomorrow and each account is exactly where you left it.

FAQ

Good questions.

How does BoxBox keep accounts separate?

Every box has its own cookies, local storage, caches and other site data. A tab in your Work box and a tab in your Personal box can be open on the same site at the same moment, and neither can see the other.

Is my data safe?

BoxBox has no servers and no telemetry, so there is nowhere for your data to go. Everything stays in your browser profile, and deleting a box deletes its data. The code is open source if you want to check.

Which browsers are supported?

Chrome and Firefox on desktop, plus Helium. On Firefox a box appears as a coloured container; on Chrome it appears as a coloured tab group.

Why does Chrome show “BoxBox started debugging this browser”?

That bar is the price of isolating accounts in Chrome, and it only appears on tabs that are inside a box. If you close it, BoxBox blocks that tab instead of quietly falling back to your normal session, and the popup offers a button to restore isolation.

Can I use a proxy with a box?

Yes. Each box can have its own HTTP, HTTPS or SOCKS5 proxy, handy for testing from another region or keeping one identity on its own network.

What happens when I delete a box?

Its cookies, storage and caches are erased with it. Your other boxes and your normal browsing are not touched.

Does it work in private windows?

BoxBox leaves private windows alone.

Is it really free?

Yes. BoxBox is free software under the GNU GPL v3, with no paid tier. If you like it, you can support its development.
